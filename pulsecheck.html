Project Overview

PulseCheck is a personal project and V1 experiment built during a hackathon. It addresses the specific communication needs of temporary teams who need to coordinate quickly without the overhead of traditional collaboration tools.

The tool provides a simple, real-time interface for team stand-ups where everyone can share progress, next steps, and blockers instantly through a shared link.

Why PulseCheck Existed

During hackathons and short-term collaborations, tools like Slack and Discord introduce unnecessary friction. They require setup, onboarding, and familiarity that slow teams down in time-sensitive environments.

Many teams only need a fast, focused way to align on progress for a short period. PulseCheck was created as a lightweight alternative that eliminates these barriers to quick collaboration.

Problem Being Solved

Short-term teams lack a simple way to see who is working on what in real time. Existing tools are designed for ongoing collaboration and become too heavy for temporary use.

  • Setup and onboarding create coordination overhead
  • Poor visibility leads to duplicated work
  • Team members waste time coordinating instead of building
  • Complex interfaces distract from the core need: alignment

Goals and Success Criteria

  • Enable real-time progress sharing without accounts or setup
  • Reduce coordination friction for hackathon teams
  • Provide structured communication to improve clarity
  • Deliver a functional V1 within hackathon time constraints
  • Validate with a real team during active collaboration

My Role

I owned both product thinking and execution for this project. This included identifying the problem space, scoping the V1, designing the user experience, building the front-end interface, and implementing a lightweight backend to persist and sync updates.

The goal was to create a complete, working product that could be tested and validated within the hackathon timeframe.

Key Product Decisions

  • No authentication: Eliminated account creation to reduce friction
  • Structured inputs: Used specific fields instead of open chat to improve clarity
  • Real-time sharing: Enabled instant visibility for all participants
  • Single link access: Made joining as simple as clicking a link
  • Limited scope: Focused on core functionality to deliver a working V1 quickly

Solution Breakdown

The interface focuses on four key questions that matter most during short-term collaboration:

  • What is your name and role?
  • What did you work on?
  • What are you working on next?
  • Are there any blockers?

Team members enter their updates, and everyone sees changes instantly through a shared URL. No login, no setup, no configuration.

Outcome and Learnings

PulseCheck was tested and actively used by my hackathon team during live collaboration. Sharing one link enabled quick alignment without any onboarding or explanation.

Key learnings from this experiment:

  • Simplicity matters most in short-term collaboration tools
  • Structured updates significantly improve clarity over open chat
  • A focused V1 can deliver real value without feature overload
  • Reducing onboarding time directly impacts team velocity

What I Would Improve Next

Given more time, I would focus on these areas to enhance the product:

  • Add optional persistence for teams that want to track progress across days
  • Improve mobile responsiveness for on-the-go updates
  • Include a simple voting mechanism for prioritizing blockers
  • Add export functionality to capture stand-up summaries
  • Conduct more user testing with different types of short-term teams
