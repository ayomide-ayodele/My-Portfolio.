Product Strategy Case Study

LUMIT: AI-Powered Theory Answer Training

An AI-powered theory answer training platform designed to help examination candidates improve how they write and present answers using structured, marking-scheme-aligned feedback.

Role: Product Manager / Founder Focus: Education & Learning Status: Pre-launch

Overview

What LUMIT Addresses

LUMIT was created around a gap in Nigerian examination preparation. Existing edtech products heavily emphasize objective or multiple-choice practice, while students preparing for written examinations often struggle with how to structure answers, include the expected key points, and use subject-specific terminology.

Problem 1

Knowing the content is not enough

Students may understand a subject but still struggle to communicate their knowledge in the format examination marking schemes reward. Structured answer writing is a distinct skill that content knowledge alone does not guarantee.

Problem 2

Theory practice lacks structured feedback

Students can practice questions, but getting specific feedback on missing points, answer structure, and terminology is difficult without a teacher or marker. Most self-study approaches cannot provide this level of detailed response analysis.

Problem 3

Existing digital preparation is heavily objective-focused

Most established Nigerian examination-preparation products emphasize multiple-choice practice, leaving theory answer training underserved. The written-response skill gap remains largely unaddressed by available digital tools.

Problem 4

Feedback is difficult to scale

Written answers require detailed review, making repeated personalized feedback difficult to provide consistently and affordably. Teachers face practical limits on how many detailed answer reviews they can complete for individual students.

Learning Context

The Examination Landscape

Understanding the stakes and structure of the target examinations that shaped LUMIT's product decisions.

Target Users

Primary: SS3 students aged approximately 15 to 18 preparing for WAEC or NECO examinations.

Secondary: WAEC and NECO resit candidates aged approximately 18 to 22 who are returning to examination preparation and need repeated practice with structured feedback.

Why Structured Answer Writing Matters

WAEC and NECO are high-stakes examinations where students are not only expected to know content but also to communicate answers in ways that align with marking schemes. The 2025 WASSCE candidate population data underscores the scale of this need: a large student population competing for limited tertiary education opportunities, where marginal improvements in theory answer quality can significantly impact overall results.

Marking schemes reward precise terminology, logical structure, and the inclusion of specific key points. Students who understand the subject matter but cannot present it in the expected format often underperform relative to their actual knowledge.

Research & Insights

Understanding the User

Research conducted to validate the problem and inform product direction.

400+

Research participants

Primary Groups

Secondary school and university students

Methods

Structured surveys, in-depth interviews, observational study

Content knowledge does not predict answer quality

Students who performed well on multiple-choice assessments often struggled when asked to produce structured written responses to the same topics. The gap between knowing and communicating was consistent across subjects.

Marking scheme awareness is low

Many students could not articulate what examiners look for in theory answers. They understood the subject but lacked clarity on how marks are allocated across key points, structure, and terminology.

Existing digital tools do not fill the gap

Students who used popular Nigerian exam preparation apps reported heavy reliance on multiple-choice practice modes. Theory answer support, where it existed, was limited to static model answers without personalized feedback.

Feedback is the most valued but least accessible resource

Students consistently rated detailed answer feedback as the most helpful form of exam preparation, but also the most difficult to access. Teacher availability, cost of private tutoring, and time constraints were cited as persistent barriers.

Product Principles

These principles were derived from the research and guide LUMIT's product decisions:

Feedback should teach, not just score

The product should help students understand what was missing and how to improve, rather than simply assigning a grade.

Evaluation should follow the marking scheme

Feedback should be grounded in the specific marking logic of each question rather than generic AI commentary.

Practice should reflect the examination context

Timed practice and structured answer writing should help students prepare for the actual conditions they face.

Accessibility matters

The product should provide useful examination preparation at a price accessible to Nigerian students and families.

Solution Design

How LUMIT Works

The core product experience: from question selection to structured, actionable feedback.

Core Product Workflow

The student journey through LUMIT is designed to mirror and improve upon real examination practice:

Select Question

Choose a theory question from the structured, verified question bank aligned to WAEC and NECO syllabi.

Write or Type Answer

Write the answer on paper and photograph it, or type directly into the application.

OCR & Review

OCR extracts handwritten text. The student reviews and corrects the extracted content before submission.

AI Evaluation

The evaluation engine assesses the answer against the specific marking scheme for that question.

Structured Feedback

Receive score breakdown, present key points, missing key points, structural feedback, and a model answer.

Performance Tracking

Review subject-level progress over time, identifying strengths and consistent areas for improvement.

Improve & Repeat

Apply the feedback to subsequent practice attempts, progressively closing the gap between current and target performance.

Supporting Product Capabilities

Timed Practice

Simulate examination conditions with configurable time limits per question.

Verified Question Bank

Past questions aligned to WAEC and NECO syllabi with verified marking schemes.

Progress Tracking

Monitor improvement across subjects and identify consistent areas of difficulty.

Subject Performance History

Detailed breakdown of performance patterns within and across subjects over time.

Product Thinking

Strategic Trade-offs

Key product decisions made under resource constraints and how they shaped the product direction.

Android first

The initial MVP is focused on Android rather than attempting to launch across every platform immediately. This reflects the device landscape of the target market and allows the team to concentrate engineering resources on a single, high-impact platform.

Six subjects at launch

The product starts with six baseline subjects rather than attempting to cover the entire WAEC and NECO curriculum immediately. This enables deeper quality per subject, more thorough marking scheme validation, and faster iteration before expanding the subject catalog.

Existing AI models instead of building from scratch

LUMIT uses OCR and existing LLM APIs rather than attempting to build a foundational model. This decision prioritizes evaluation accuracy and product experience over infrastructure ownership, and allows the team to focus on the product layer where differentiation matters most.

Lean technical execution

Engineering and specialized technical work are planned through vetted project-based contractors while product direction remains founder-led. This keeps the team structure aligned with the current stage while maintaining quality on critical technical components.

Accuracy before scale

AI evaluation accuracy is treated as the most important quality metric, with pre-launch validation against human marker scores required before any public release. The product will not ship until evaluation reliability meets a defined threshold, even if that extends the timeline.

Reflection

What Building LUMIT Taught Me

Personal product management reflections from the founder perspective.

On Product Ownership

Building LUMIT from the ground up has been a practical education in product ownership that goes far beyond feature definition. As founder and PM, I have been responsible not only for what the product does but for the decisions about what it does not do, at least not yet. This means living with the tension between the full vision and the scoped reality of an MVP.

One of the most important lessons has been learning to separate the product ambition from the launch product. The long-term vision for LUMIT is expansive, but the launch product must solve a specific problem for a specific user group with enough depth to matter. Holding that line requires constant discipline, especially when every conversation with potential users reveals another valuable feature that could be built.

On Problem Validation

The research process reinforced something that is easy to state but difficult to internalize: the problem you think exists is not necessarily the problem users experience. Initial assumptions about what students struggle with were refined, and in some cases replaced, by what the research actually surfaced. The gap between content knowledge and structured answer presentation was real, but its specific shape, its relationship to marking scheme awareness, and its emotional dimension for students facing high-stakes examinations were all more nuanced than early hypotheses suggested.

This has made me a stronger advocate for research that is genuinely open to being wrong. Confirmation-seeking research is easy to conduct and dangerous to rely on. The most valuable insights from LUMIT's research came from listening to what did not fit the initial framework.

On Working Under Constraints

Resource constraints shaped LUMIT in ways that I now recognize as product-defining rather than merely limiting. The decision to focus on Android only, to launch with six subjects, to use existing AI infrastructure rather than building proprietary models, and to run a lean engineering operation through vetted contractors were not compromises made reluctantly. They were strategic choices that allowed the product to exist at all at this stage.

What I have learned is that constraints, when accepted early and internalized fully, become a framework for clarity. They eliminate options that are not genuinely available and force sharper thinking about the options that remain. The product is better for having been built within real limits rather than designed as though resources were unlimited.

On AI as a Product Concern

LUMIT depends on AI evaluation accuracy, and that dependency has shaped how I think about AI in product management. AI reliability is not purely a technical problem to be solved by engineering. It is a product concern that affects user trust, the perceived value of feedback, and ultimately whether students and parents believe the product is worth paying for.

This has meant treating evaluation accuracy as the primary quality metric, with pre-launch validation against human marker scores as a non-negotiable gate. It has also meant designing the product experience to set appropriate expectations: the AI is positioned as a practice and improvement tool, not as a replacement for teacher judgment. Managing that perception is a product decision, not a technical one.
